The 2026 Iran war began on February 28, 2026, when U.S. and Israeli forces launched massive joint air strikes on Iranian military and nuclear infrastructure. The opening wave of attacks resulted in the death of Iran's Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, triggering widespread regional escalation, retaliatory strikes across the Middle East, and an ongoing naval standoff in the Strait of Hormuz. [1, 2]
Origins and Early Escalation

• Initial Strikes: Codenamed Operation Epic Fury, U.S. and Israeli forces carried out hundreds of strikes targeting Iranian air defenses, missile sites, and leadership compounds.
• Leadership Shift: Following the death of Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, Mojtaba Khamenei was appointed as the new supreme leader amid intense political and military realignment within the regime.
• Regional Retaliation: Iran launched retaliatory drone and missile barrages targeting U.S. military assets and regional infrastructure across the Persian Gulf, alongside renewed clashes involving regional proxy networks. [2]

The Strait of Hormuz Crisis

• Naval Blockade: A central feature of the conflict involves contesting the Strait of Hormuz, a critical global choke point for petroleum transit.
• Control Disputes: The U.S. and Iran have engaged in competing enforcement and blockades, with Iran asserting authorization rights over remaining maritime traffic.
• Diplomatic Efforts: Mediation attempts, including a Pakistan-mediated arrangement and subsequent talks hosted through Oman, have struggled to secure a permanent maritime and geopolitical settlement. [1, 4, 5]

Broader Impacts and Current Status

• Regional Fronts: Periodic flare-ups continue to affect neighboring areas, including localized Israeli strikes in southern Lebanon and ongoing security incidents involving Red Sea shipping routes.
• Global Energy and Logistics: Disruption to shipping lanes through the Gulf has kept international energy markets volatile, prompting continuous monitoring by the International Energy Agency and major economic powers.
• Military Strain: Long-term naval deployments and high utilization rates of precision interceptors continue to test defense inventories and force readiness for the participating nations. [8, 9]
